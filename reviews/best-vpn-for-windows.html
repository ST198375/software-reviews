Best VPN for Windows: what to check before you subscribe

Windows is where most people first try a VPN — and where a bad choice is most obvious, because every protocol switch and every dropped connection is visible.

What matters on Windows specifically

  • Kill switch that actually works. Test it: block traffic, then pull the network cable. If anything leaks, the VPN is decorative.
  • Split tunnelling. Lets your banking app and local printer bypass the tunnel. Without it, half your work tools break.
  • WireGuard-based protocol. Noticeably faster than OpenVPN on the same connection.
  • System-level DNS. A VPN that leaks DNS requests gives away every site you visit.
  • Does it start with Windows? A VPN you forget to switch on protects nothing.

The most common mistake is judging a VPN by its speed test on a local server. Real use happens on distant servers, in the evening, when everyone is streaming. Test then.

FAQ

Does Windows have a built-in VPN? Yes, but it is a plain tunnel with no kill switch, no obfuscation and no server network — fine for a corporate connection, not for consumer privacy.

Free VPN or paid? Free from a vendor that sells paid plans is usable for testing. Free from an unknown vendor usually means your traffic is the product.
