Best video editors for beginners

Every beginner video editor is free until you want to publish. That is the pattern worth knowing before you install anything: the free tier gets a watermark, an export cap or a short time limit. Publishing is where free stops.

If you want to cut a clip today

Filmora exists for this. The timeline behaves like the paid editors, the auto-cut and noise removal tools do the boring parts, and nobody expects you to understand colour spaces first. The trade-off is money: it is a subscription product, and the trial pushes a watermark onto your export until you pay. Check whether the one-off licence suits you better than a yearly plan — for occasional use it usually does.

Free forever

CapCut is the strongest free editor for social-length video, and DaVinci Resolve is the strongest free editor for anything longer. Resolve is the one people underestimate: the free version would have cost four figures a decade ago. It also asks for a modern graphics card and a weekend of patience before it feels natural. CapCut is easier, and it has a second cost: the company is ByteDance, and several governments have questioned where user footage and face data end up. If your clips are of your kids, that matters more than a transition pack.

Occasional editors

Clipchamp in a browser is enough, provided you accept the Microsoft account and the export limits. For Mac users, iMovie is already installed and covers trimming, titles and music. Neither is a good reason to pay for anything. Try both before spending.

Verdict: beginners pay for time saved, not for features. If you publish weekly, a paid editor pays for itself in one avoided evening. If you publish twice a year, the free options are not a compromise — they are the correct answer.

Before you buy any editor, export a one-minute clip with a title, a music track and a cut. That single test exposes the watermark rule, the export cap and how the timeline feels — faster than any review, including this one.

FAQ

Do beginner editors run on old laptops? CapCut and Filmora do reasonably well on integrated graphics if you work at 1080p. Resolve is the one that will make an old machine feel old.

Is a watermark on the trial normal? Yes, and it is not a bug. Assume the trial is for learning the interface, not for publishing.

Which editor is best for phones? CapCut, with iMovie a close second on iOS.
